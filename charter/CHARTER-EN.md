Part I: The Foundational Declaration

Preface
The world is founded upon wisdom. From galaxies to fundamental particles, a single, all-pervading order flows. Human beings have the capacity to comprehend this order and to arrange their lives in accordance with it. This is humanity's eternal mission: the pursuit of wisdom.
In three realms, humankind has always searched for meaning:
1. The outer world (nature)
1. The inner world (the human being)
1. The connecting world (reason and wisdom)

Whenever people have established harmony among these three realms, peace, justice and flourishing have arisen. Whenever this bond has been severed, war, ruin, poverty and crisis have cast their shadow.
Today, humanity faces a deep and multidimensional crisis:
1. The collapse of ethics and trust
1. Pervasive injustice
1. The destruction of the environment
1. A crisis of meaning and identity
1. New forms of digital exploitation
1. The fracturing of the family

These crises do not stem from a shortage of knowledge; they stem from a shortage of wisdom.
Knowledge is a tool; wisdom determines the direction of movement and the manner in which the tool is used.
Why Wisdom?
Wisdom is the wellspring of balance between the material and the spiritual worlds. Wisdom guides human beings in transforming knowledge into justice, power into responsibility, freedom into dignity, and wealth into transcendence.
Wisdom is the common language of humanity, the earth and the heavens: a language that binds cultures, religions, nations and generations to one another.
Wisdom is the only force that can:
1. Mend the rift between the individual and society
1. Heal the rift between humanity and nature
1. Unite the rift between past and future
1. Turn the rift between science and ethics into a mutual bond

Wisdom is neither a religious belief nor a mere philosophical theory; it is a way of living, a path of civilization-building, and a creation-centered order.
Why a Charter?
To pass through the crises of the world, humanity needs a mother document: a document that would:
1.	Reconcile human beings with existence
2.	Chart the path from knowledge → reason → wisdom → justice → sustainable civilization
3.	Guarantee the fundamental rights of the human being, the family, the child, nature and the generations
4.	Order economic, political, cultural and technological systems upon justice, responsibility and reason
5.	Free human beings from subjugation, modern slavery, dependency and heedlessness
6.	Entrust the future not to the hands of power, but to collective reason and a worldwide bond among peoples

Philosophical Definitions
Socrates: Wisdom is "knowing one's own ignorance."
Plato: Wisdom is equivalent to inner justice.
Aristotle: The distinction between "theoretical wisdom" (Sophia) and "practical wisdom" (Phronesis).
Farabi: Knowledge of the truths of beings and action in accordance with what is fitting.
Avicenna: The perfection of the human soul through the conception of intelligibles and the affirmation of truths.
Suhrawardi: Illuminationist wisdom, a synthesis of demonstration and mysticism.
Mulla Sadra: The human being's becoming an intellectual world resembling the objective world.
Kant: The harmony of the will with the ultimate end (the highest good).
Heidegger: Openness toward Being.
Hannah Arendt: The capacity to think from the standpoint of others and to judge public affairs.
Religious Definitions
Islam: The Qur'an calls wisdom "abundant good" and presents the prophets as teachers of the Book and of wisdom.
Christianity: Wisdom as a gift of the Holy Spirit.
Judaism: Wisdom is the fear of God and turning away from evil.
Zoroastrianism: "Asha" as the true order and the wisdom that governs existence.
Hinduism: "Veda," meaning knowledge, and "Vedanta," the knowledge of Brahman and Atman.
Buddhism: "Prajñā" as one of the perfections (pāramitā).
Taoism: The "Tao" as the way or path of existence.
Mystical Definitions
Ibn Arabi: Wisdom is "divine light" shining in the heart of the saint.
Rumi: Wisdom is "knowing by the light of God."
Ramana Maharshi: Wisdom is self-knowledge (ātma-vichāra, self-inquiry).
Khosravani Wisdom: Knowledge through intuition and the inner illumination of the heart.
Scientific Definitions
Contemporary psychology: Wisdom as a combination of knowledge, insight, empathy, emotional regulation, resilience and the capacity for judgment.
The balance theory of wisdom (Baltes and Sternberg): The capacity to apply knowledge and experience toward the common good.
Complexity science: Wisdom as the capacity of a system to maintain dynamic equilibrium and to adapt to change.
Ecology: Ecosystems operate on the basis of nature's wisdom.
Literary and Artistic Definitions
Ferdowsi: "Mighty is the one who is wise."
Hafez (Ghazal 3): "Speak of the minstrel and the wine; seek less the secrets of the age, / for no one has unlocked, nor will unlock, this riddle by wisdom."
Saadi: "The children of Adam are limbs of a single body."
Rumi (Masnavi, Book One): "Knowledge and wisdom are born of the lawful morsel; / love and tenderness come from the lawful morsel."
Shakespeare: "To thine own self be true."
Social and Political Definitions
Wisdom in society: The capacity of a society to resolve conflicts peacefully and to build solidarity and trust.
Wise governance: A system in which decisions are made on the basis of collective reason, foresight, transparency, participation and accountability.
Justice as wisdom: The capacity to establish balance between freedom and equality, between the individual and society, and between short-term and long-term interests.
Comprehensive Definition and Meta-Synthesis
Wisdom is the highest degree of knowing and of living; a divine-human power in which:
1. Knowledge attains an understanding of the truth of existence
1. Ethics puts this knowledge to work in individual and social action
1. Purification of the soul frees the human being from attachments and illusions
1. Balance and harmony are established among the many dimensions of being

Wisdom is not the accumulation of information but the ability to distinguish the genuine from the counterfeit; not mere analytical power but the art of living well; not an abstract theory but a practical path toward the elevation of the individual and of civilization.
Operational Definition
Operational Definition (for Measurement and Implementation)
"Wisdom is the ability to recognize, evaluate and choose decisions and actions that take into account their ethical, systemic, long-term and intergenerational consequences, and that prefer sustainable collective good over short-term interests."
This definition is the basis for evaluating all the principles, policies and recommendations of this Pact.

	Social and civilizational wisdom

No level is complete without the others.

Part IV: The Foundational Principles
Principle Zero through Principle 15

Principle Zero: Wisdom, the Origin and Purpose of Existence
Wisdom is the origin and purpose of existence, and the guide by which human beings realize themselves and bring prosperity to the Earth.
Note 1 (ontological dimension). The world is built upon wisdom, and no phenomenon in it is without purpose or order.
Note 2 (anthropological dimension). The human being possesses a wisdom-seeking nature. Wisdom is the flourishing of these innate capacities.
Note 3 (methodological dimension). Attaining wisdom requires the simultaneous use of reason, intuition and experience, together with the benefit of revealed sources.
Note 4 (practical dimension). Every principle that appears in this Charter must be interpreted and implemented in the light of Principle Zero. All decisions must be weighed against the question: "Is this decision wise?"
Principle 1: The Dignity of the Earth
The Earth holds rights; the human being is its trustee.
1.	The Earth is the common mother of all human beings, all living beings and all future generations.
2.	Absolute ownership of the Earth is impossible; each individual has a right of use only to the extent of his or her livelihood, labor and need.
3.	The destruction of the Earth, the ruin of resources and pollution are transgressions against the right of the mother of existence and a violation of the Charter of Wisdom.
Article 1. Ownership is defined on the basis of "share of livelihood + share of labor + social justice."
Article 2. Use is permitted only within the framework of sustainable development, bioethics and intergenerational justice.
Clause 1. The destruction of soil, water, air, forests and ecosystems is a crime under wisdom and a violation of the Charter.
Clause 2. Unsustainable exploitation is a betrayal of future generations.
Note 1. The recycling of resources and the circular economy are a structural duty of the institutions of wisdom.
Note 2. Public education in "Earth literacy" is compulsory in all societies.
Note 3. Industrial, agricultural, energy and urban projects lacking a wisdom-centered environmental assessment have no legitimacy.
Principle 2: Protecting and Safeguarding the Environment
Life is one; humanity is its guardian.
1.	The human being is responsible for preserving biodiversity, both animal and plant.
2.	Development is legitimate only when it is in harmony with respect for the Earth.
Article 1. Deliberate or organized harm to the environment constitutes a "transgression against the pillar of the Earth."
Article 2. Developmental activities must be consistent with scientific criteria, bioethics and wisdom.
Clause 1. Urban planning must consider its "hundred-year" effects on the Earth, not short-term interests.
Clause 2. The development of clean technologies and new energies is a duty of contemporary civilization.
Note 1. Digital tools for large-scale monitoring of the Earth (Wisdom AI, Wisdom OS, Wisdom Chain) must be developed.
Note 2. Public participation in protecting the Earth is both a right and a duty.
Principle 3: Justice and the Sharing of the Earth's Resources
The bounty of the Earth belongs to all generations, not to a single class.
1.	All human beings have an equal right to enjoy life-giving resources.
2.	No state or class has the right to monopolize natural resources.
Article 1. The distribution of resources must be based on need, share of livelihood, social justice and the benefit of future generations.
Article 2. Monopoly over resources in any form violates justice and wisdom.
Clause 1. History has shown that civilizations that managed the Earth justly have been durable and prosperous.
Clause 2. Religious and mystical texts emphasize justice on the Earth and the ethical use of its resources.
Note 1. Future generations hold a "time-bound right of ownership" over resources.
Note 2. The destruction of future resources is a betrayal of humanity and contrary to wisdom.
Principle 4: The Dignity of the Human Being
The human being: mirror of the Earth, child of spacetime, bearer of the message of awareness and responsibility.
1.	Human dignity takes precedence over every law, economy or ideology.
2.	No system without respect for the human being has legitimacy.
Article 1. From the moment of birth, every human being possesses inherent dignity, the right to life, the right to growth, the right to security, and a capacity for receiving wisdom.
Article 2. No distinction, discrimination or deprivation is permitted on the basis of the circumstances of birth, family, race, gender, religion, nationality, class or geographic location.
Clause 1. The child is a trust of humanity and a potential bearer of the wisdom of the future; the child is not the property of parents, the state, an ideology or the market.
Clause 2. Any violence, discrimination, exploitation or structural neglect toward a child is a flagrant violation of human dignity and contrary to wisdom.
Note 1. Ethical, rational, social and spiritual education from the beginning of life is the right of the child and the duty of the society of wisdom.
Note 2. Humanity has the right to benefit from technology (AI) without exploitation and abuse.
Principle 5: Birth and Death
Birth and death are the two thresholds of human dignity and wisdom.
Section 1: Birth
1.	Every human birth creates a shared responsibility for the family, society and the structure of wisdom to preserve the person's dignity, health, education and all-round growth.
2.	The right to a worthy life, physical and mental health, wise education, security, affection and the opportunity to flourish are inalienable rights of every human being from the beginning of birth.
Section 2: Death
1.	Death is not the end of a human being's worth, but the end of biological presence and the beginning of the spiritual, ethical and historical evaluation of that person's legacy.
2.	Until the last moment of life, the human being possesses dignity and the right to respect, care, meaning and a humane farewell.
3.	No power has the right to make a person's death an instrument of interrogation, intimidation, domination, commerce or ideological propaganda.
Section 3: Life Between the Two Thresholds
1.	Human life is a conscious interval between birth and death for the realization of reason, ethics, justice and meaning.
2.	The worth of a human being is measured not by the length of life but by the depth of wisdom, the quality of ethics and the degree of one's impact on the world.
3.	Every human being is responsible for turning his or her birth into an opportunity for growth and his or her death into a legacy of wisdom.
Principle 6: Human Responsibility Toward the Earth, the World and Others
1.	The human being is responsible for the Earth, society, the environment and future generations.
2.	Ethical responsibility and awareness are the measure of human growth.
3.	Every human being is a partner in worldwide justice.
Article 1. The human being has a duty to transmit knowledge, experience and ethics to the generations.
Article 2. Every human being must answer for his or her actions to conscience, society and the future.
Article 3. The mechanisms of wisdom (Wisdom Chain) are instruments for measuring and making transparent this responsibility.
Clause 1. Ignorance and fanaticism are the historic enemies of humanity.
Clause 2. Injustice toward one human being is injustice toward all humanity.
Clause 3. Every individual must strive for peace, justice and truth.
Principle 7: Human Justice and Universal Equality
1.	Human beings are equal in dignity.
2.	Justice is the foundation of all human relations and of political structure.
3.	No human being should be made a victim of poverty, war, ignorance or discrimination.
Article 1. Opportunities must be distributed justly and without monopoly.
Article 2. The economic system must not turn the human being into an instrument of production or a slave of capital.
Article 3. Every human being has the right to a minimum of honor and dignity.
Clause 1. The history of civilizations has proved that injustice is the root of collapse.
Clause 2. Supremacism and inequality are deviations from the path of wisdom.
Clause 3. The Wisdom network must be an instrument of financial justice.
Note 1. The rights of future generations in human and natural resources are fixed.
Note 2. War, aggression and occupation are the destruction of humanity and a violation of the Charter.
Note 3. The institutions of wisdom are obliged to monitor social justice.
Principle 8: The Right to Health and Treatment
1.	Human health is part of inherent human dignity.
2.	Every human being has the right to access health of body, mind and spirit.
3.	Access to treatment, prevention and medical care is equal and essential for all human beings.
Article 1. Every human being has the right to equal and ethical health and medical care.
Article 2. Prevention and the preservation of health are the duty of the individual, society and the state.
Article 3. Mental and physical health are a condition for active participation in society and for the realization of practical wisdom.
Clause 1. Access to safe water, clean air, wholesome food and a healthy environment is part of the right to health.
Clause 2. Treatment must not be limited by social class, income or power.
Clause 3. Illness and the poverty of health are a violation of justice and human dignity.
Note 1. States are obliged to establish a universal system of prevention and treatment.
Note 2. The medical and therapeutic sciences must be in harmony with ethics, justice and wisdom.
Note 3. The health of body, mind and society is a condition for the human being's effective participation in practical wisdom.
Principle 9: The Law of Motion and Transformation in Spacetime
1.	Existence is built upon continuous motion in the field of spacetime.
2.	No being has absolute stillness; stillness is equivalent to death, and motion to life.
3.	The human being must order the path of his or her growth through an understanding of the laws of motion, change and transformation.
Article 1. Every human structure, whether individual, social, cultural or political, must be in harmony with the principle of transformation and evolution.
Article 2. Resistance to the natural flow of transformation leads to corruption, ruin, stagnation and the collapse of civilizations.
Clause 1. All phenomena take shape in a network of cause and effect within the fabric of spacetime.
Clause 2. Development must be aligned with the natural rhythm of the world and the laws of transformation.
Note 1. The institutions of wisdom are obliged to monitor and analyze future-shaping changes in the world.
Note 2. Every policy, law or structure must be built upon dynamism and the capacity to evolve.
Note 3. Cultural, scientific or political stagnation is considered a violation of the pillar of spacetime.
Principle 10: The Bond of History, Present and Future
1.	Time is not linear; it is a ring joining past, present and future.
2.	Humanity becomes wise only when it understands the complete cycle of time and learns from it.
Article 1. The study of history and the analysis of the future are the two wings of theoretical wisdom.
Article 2. Individual and social reason without historical memory is incomplete.
Clause 1. Historical ignorance has been the source of the fall of civilizations.
Clause 2. Scientific futures studies must be part of national and worldwide planning.
Note 1. Cultural institutions are obliged to safeguard the historical memory of nations.
Note 2. The teaching of history must be wisdom-centered, comparative and universal, not political and selective.
Principle 11: Spatial and Temporal Justice
1.	The human being is neither the center of the world nor passive within it, but the "conscious knot of spacetime."
2.	Every human behavior has a spatial and temporal effect and creates ethical responsibility.
3.	The human being must act in such a way that the spatial-temporal effects of his or her actions generate good.
Article 1. Human structures must be designed with attention to spatial justice (equal distribution of resources) and temporal justice (the right of future generations).
Article 2. The use of technology must be subject to a spacetime-centered ethics.
Clause 1. The destruction of the Earth and the ruin of resources is a violation of temporal justice.
Clause 2. Creating geographic discrimination is a violation of spatial justice.
Note 1. Future generations are holders of rights; unrestrained exploitation is prohibited.
Note 2. Architecture, urban planning and technology must keep space harmonious and healthy for human beings.
Principle 12: The Most Pure Water
Water, the root of the purity of creation.
Article 1 – Philosophical definition. The Most Pure Water is the manifestation of "primordial purity" and the bearer of the "capacity for receptivity and nurture."
Article 2 – Scientific definition. Water is a compound of two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom, in continuous transformation within the cycle of the biosphere.
Article 3 – Legal definition. The Most Pure Water is a "fundamental cosmic right." No state, corporation, institution or individual holds absolute ownership of water. Water is the common heritage of the Earth.
Article 4 – Social definition. All great civilizations formed beside a river, a sea or a spring. Without water, no culture and no human city endures.
Principle 12-1: The Universal Right to Water
Water is the inherent right of all beings; no national or international law may deprive or limit this right.
Principle 12-2: The Prohibition of Water Pollution
Any pollution of water resources is regarded as a transgression against the right to life.
Principle 12-3: Water Justice
The unjust distribution of water is a form of "environmental apartheid." No region has the right to deprive poorer regions of water.
Principle 12-4: The Water Cycle, the Vital Pulse of the Earth
The cycle of precipitation, evaporation, infiltration and flow is the circulatory system of the Earth.
Principle 12-5: Protecting Rivers, Seas and Underground Aquifers
The prohibition of any destructive diversion, excessive extraction, destructive dam-building, and the sale of water as property.
Principle 12-6: Reviving Dead Waters
States are obliged to revive wetlands, polluted rivers and dried-up lakes.
Principle 12-7: Water and Health
Water scarcity and water pollution are directly linked to disease, collective stress, forced migration and social collapse.
Principle 12-8: Water and Ethics
The Most Pure Water is a symbol of inner purification. "The human heart, too, must be clear like water."
Principle 12-9: Water and the Economy
Water is the foundation of food production, industry, energy, health and development. Without water, no sustainable economy can take shape.
Principle 12-10: Water and Time
Rising temperatures, melting ice, declining rainfall and the destruction of rivers are the greatest challenges of the third millennium.
Principle 12-11: Water Foresight
States are obliged to draw up fifty-year and hundred-year water plans.
Principle 12-12: Water and Intergenerational Justice
Today's water belongs to tomorrow's generations as well.
Principle 12-13: Integrated Water Management
Scientific, participatory, non-political and transparent control.
Principle 12-14: New Technologies
Desalination methods, advanced treatment plants, remote sensing, and AI management.
Principle 12-15: Wisdom-Centered Water Economics
Investment in water must rest on sustainability, justice and transparency.
Principle 12-16: The Universal Pact on Water
The nations of the world pledge that they will:
1.	Never make water a political weapon
2.	Never partition rivers among nations
3.	Regard pollution as an international crime
4.	Make water the first priority of rescue in times of crisis
The Oath of the Most Pure Water
"By the Most Pure Water, by the first life, by the tears of mothers, by the blood of the martyrs on the path of justice, we swear that we will preserve water, keep it clean, and regard it as the common heritage of all human beings and all generations."
Principle 13: The Soil of Beneficence
Soil, the root of being, the bed of nurture and the cradle of justice.
Article 1 – Philosophical definition. The Soil of Beneficence is the "ground of manifestation" and the "point of embodiment"; the substance that enables the Most Pure Water, the light of knowledge and the breeze of wisdom to bear fruit.
Article 2 – Scientific definition. Soil is a complex assemblage of minerals, organic matter, water, air and millions of microorganisms. One centimeter of fertile soil takes on average 100 to 300 years to form. Ninety-five percent of the world's food is produced, directly or indirectly, from soil.
Article 3 – Legal definition. The Soil of Beneficence is the common heritage of the Earth, and not state, personal or corporate property. Ownership of soil is "conditional ownership," not absolute.
Article 4 – Social definition. Soil is the birthplace of civilization, agriculture, settlement and human community. Every society that dishonored the soil saw its civilization fall.
Principle 13-1: The Soil's Right to Life
Soil possesses a "right to life" and must be protected from destruction, pollution, excessive pesticide spraying, destructive plowing and the annihilation of its microorganisms.
Principle 13-2: Human Responsibility Toward Soil
Every citizen, institution, state and corporation is obliged to preserve, restore and revive the soil.
Principle 13-3: The Prohibition of Selling Absolute Ownership of Soil
No state or institution has the right to sell soil as absolute property. Only a "conditional right of use" may be transferred.
Principle 13-4: Soil, the Memory of the Earth
Soil has layers that record within themselves the millions-of-years-long history of the Earth.
Principle 13-5: The Prohibition of Reckless Land-Use Change
The destruction of farmland, forests and rangelands is prohibited.
Principle 13-6: Restoring Damaged Soils
States are obliged to restore all degraded lands by the year 2050.
Principle 13-7: Soil and Health
Micronutrient deficiency in soil = increased disease. Soil pollution = cancer, infertility, hormonal disorders, neurological diseases. Agricultural toxins = a threat to the human species.
Principle 13-8: Soil and the Economy
Food security, agriculture, animal husbandry, plant medicine, pharmaceuticals, the textile industry and architecture all depend on soil.
Principle 13-9: Soil and Culture
Soil is part of the identity of nations. Karbala, Tus, Persepolis, Ur, Athens, Jerusalem, Machu Picchu. Every soil is the cultural memory of a people.
Principle 13-10: Soil, a Product of Time
The formation of soil is the work of 10,000 to 100,000 years. Its destruction within a few years is therefore a "theft from time."
Principle 13-11: Soil and the Future
Climate change, desertification, drought and dust storms are directly linked to soil erosion.
Principle 13-12: Intergenerational Justice
The Soil of Beneficence is the right of future generations.
Principle 13-13: Wisdom-Centered Agriculture
The prohibition of carcinogenic toxins, the promotion of regenerative agriculture, the preservation of soil microorganisms, and biodiversity.
Principle 13-14: Smart Soil Technologies
Remote sensing, precision mapping, artificial intelligence for predicting erosion, and low-water cultivation.
Principle 13-15: Wisdom-Centered Soil Architecture
The use of soil in sustainable architecture (mud brick, straw-clay plaster, wet-and-dry techniques, the traditional structures of Iran).
Principle 13-16: The Universal Pact on the Beneficence of Soil
The nations pledge to:
1.	Bring soil erosion to zero by 2050
2.	Prevent destructive agriculture
3.	Halt deforestation
4.	Push back desertification
5.	Make soil the axis of food security
The Oath of the Soil of Beneficence
"By the Soil of Beneficence, by Mother Earth, by the cradle of life, we swear that we will keep the soil well, that we will not defile it or plunder it, and that we will make every span of it a bed of justice, of life, and of nurture for the humanity of the future."
Principle 14: The Fire of Knowledge
Fire, the light of reason, the engine of transformation and the guarantor of empowerment.
Article 1 – Philosophical definition. The Fire of Knowledge: "the illuminating force that, through the understanding of the systems of the world, raises collective awareness and makes possible informed and ethical choices."
Article 2 – Scientific definition. The Fire of Knowledge includes:
1. The production and reproduction of basic and applied knowledge (basic sciences, engineering, the humanities)
1. Enabling technologies (information, biotechnology, energy, materials)
1. Research infrastructure (laboratories, universities, innovation centers)

Article 3 – Legal definition. Knowledge is the public right of humanity; exclusive ownership of "public awareness" is prohibited. States and institutions are responsible for guaranteeing equitable access to knowledge.
Article 4 – Social definition. The Fire of Knowledge is the engine of advancing welfare, justice and participation. Knowledge-centered societies increase their capacity for self-governance, resilience and creativity.
Principle 14-1: Truth-Seeking Knowledge
Knowledge must serve truth, the public good and justice, not domination, deception or exploitation.
Principle 14-2: Equitable Access to Knowledge
No monopoly, pricing or political restriction may limit disadvantaged communities' access to basic knowledge, health, education and vital technologies.
Principle 14-3: The Ethics of Research and Technology
All research must observe ethical standards of human, animal and environmental safety. High-risk research (such as genetic manipulation, biological weapons, weather and climate, nuclear) requires interdisciplinary study and international approvals.
Principle 14-4: Open Knowledge, Responsible Data and Privacy
Data must be open, accessible and auditable, while the privacy of individuals is respected.
Principle 14-5: Innovation Justice and the Distribution of Technology
Technology must be distributed for the benefit of all, especially the deprived. The monopolization of foundational technologies is prohibited.
Principle 14-6: Guaranteeing Foresight and Environmental Safety
Every major project must assess its long-term environmental, social and intergenerational consequences.
Principle 14-7: Artificial Intelligence (AI)
AI must be transparent, explainable and equipped with ethical auditing. The use of AI for repression, censorship, manipulation of information or discrimination is prohibited.
Principle 14-8: Biotechnology and Genetics
Intervention in the human genome and in wild species requires ethical frameworks and international ratification.
Principle 14-9: Energy and New Materials
The development of clean energies (fusion, renewables) has priority. The production of new materials must reduce environmental vulnerability.
Principle 14-10: Data and Networks
The worldwide internet must be secure, open and accessible. Data sovereignty should be defined on the basis of users' rights and the public commons.
Principle 14-11: Wisdom-and-Science Literacy
Public education must include data literacy, an ethical understanding of technology, and the history of science.
Principle 14-12: The Role of the Media and the Arts
The media are obliged to convey knowledge with accuracy, impartiality and in comprehensible language. Art and literature must play a bridging role between science and society.
Principle 14-13: The Knowledge Governance Network
The creation of a consultative body for world knowledge (with scientific, ethical and social representatives from all regions) to assess technologies and propose policies.
Principle 14-14: Transparency and Reporting
All major research projects must provide public and auditable reports.
Principle 14-15: Corrective Procedure
In the event of error or harm, mechanisms of restoration, compensation and correction must be activated. Corrections must be carried out on the basis of scientific evidence, ethics and the consensus of collective reason.
Principle 14-16: Special Cases
1. Defense and security research that carries the possibility of genocide or widespread harm falls under international conventions and strict oversight.
1. Intellectual property (IP) must be balanced; foundational inventions vital to public health must be subject to equitable access models.
1. Any scientific deprivation of local communities of sources of knowledge and technology is condemned as a form of exploitation.

The Oath of the Fire of Knowledge
"I, as a bearer of this Charter, swear by the Fire of Knowledge: that I will make knowledge a means of liberation and service, not an instrument of domination; that I will seek the truth and, in the pursuit of the common good, uphold the freedom of reason and justice."
Principle 15: Air – Wisdom
Air, the all-encompassing element, the spirit of the world, and the lifeline of humanity.
Article 1 – Scientific and cosmic definition. Air is not merely oxygen and nitrogen; it is a living system that regulates the cycle of life and makes possible humidity, rainfall, climate, the growth of plants, and the health of animals and human beings.
Article 2 – Religious and spiritual definition. In divine scriptures, air is linked to the "spirit." The breathing-in of the spirit is the bond between air and human life.

The master formula of the Charter:
Theoretical Wisdom + Practical Wisdom = Transcendent Wisdom
Principle 15-1: The Dominion of Wisdom over All Pillars
Water, soil, fire, time and the human being are incomplete without wisdom. This principle takes precedence over, and holds sway over, all preceding chapters.
Principle 15-2: The Responsibility to Protect Air
Protecting air = protecting the wisdom of nature. Polluting air = transgression against life.
Principle 15-3: The Responsibility to Promote Wisdom
Every individual bears an ethical and social duty: to spread, adapt and safeguard wisdom.

Part V: The Thematic Protocols
Ten Protocols

Protocol 1: Women's Rights and Gender Justice
Article 1. All human beings, regardless of gender, are equal in dignity and rights.
Article 2. Gender discrimination in law, the economy, culture, the family and politics is prohibited.
Article 3. Reproductive rights, sexual health, the education of girls, and the political and economic participation of women are guaranteed.
Article 4. Domestic violence, forced marriage, child marriage and female genital mutilation are regarded as crimes.
Article 5. The institutions of wisdom are obliged to monitor and report annually on the indicators of gender justice.
Protocol 2: Children's Rights
Article 1. Every child has the right to life, growth, education, health, security and dignity.
Article 2. Child labor, child marriage, the recruitment of children into armed conflict, and every form of exploitation are prohibited.
Article 3. Free, compulsory and quality education is guaranteed for all children.
Article 4. Children have the right to be heard in decisions that concern them.
Article 5. The institutions of wisdom are obliged to establish a system of protection for children without guardians, refugee children and children with disabilities.
Protocol 3: The Rights of Minorities
Article 1. Religious, ethnic, linguistic and sexual minorities and persons with disabilities enjoy the right to dignity, freedom and equal participation.
Article 2. Systemic discrimination, hate speech and violence against minorities are prohibited.
Article 3. The linguistic, cultural and religious rights of minorities are respected.
Article 4. Persons with disabilities have the right to equal access to public spaces, education, employment and health services.
Article 5. The institutions of wisdom are obliged to monitor and report violations of the rights of minorities.
Protocol 4: Democracy, the Rule of Law and Freedom of Expression
Article 1. Government must be built upon the consent of citizens, free and fair elections, and the rule of law.
Article 2. An independent, impartial and accountable judiciary is guaranteed.
Article 3. Freedom of expression, of the press, of assembly, of association and of religion is guaranteed.
Article 4. Political prisons, torture, execution without a fair trial and enforced disappearance are prohibited.
Article 5. The institutions of wisdom are obliged to monitor and report annually on the state of democracy and human rights.
Protocol 5: Economic Justice and Labor Rights
Article 1. A minimum wage, unemployment insurance, retirement, maternity leave and workplace safety are guaranteed.
Article 2. Independent labor unions and the right to peaceful strike are recognized.
Article 3. Monopoly, corruption, tax evasion and money laundering are prohibited.
Article 4. Progressive taxation, the redistribution of wealth, and public investment in health, education and infrastructure are encouraged.
Article 5. The institutions of wisdom are obliged to monitor and report on the indicators of economic inequality.
Protocol 6: Peace, Disarmament and Security
Article 1. Wars of aggression, occupation, terrorism and crimes against humanity are condemned.
Article 2. Nuclear, biological and chemical disarmament is the ultimate goal of humanity.
Article 3. The arms trade must be transparent, limited and subject to international oversight.
Article 4. The peaceful resolution of disputes through negotiation, mediation and international courts is encouraged.
Article 5. The institutions of wisdom are obliged to monitor and report annually on the state of peace and worldwide security.
Protocol 7: Refugees, Migrants and Climate-Displaced Persons
Article 1. Refugees have the right to asylum, security, health, education and work.
Article 2. The arbitrary detention of refugees, forced expulsion and the separation of families are prohibited.
Article 3. Climate-displaced persons are recognized as legitimate refugees.
Article 4. Host countries must benefit from international support for settlement, integration and voluntary return.
Article 5. The institutions of wisdom are obliged to monitor and report on the situation of refugees and migrants.
Protocol 8: Worldwide Health and Pandemics
Article 1. Health is a fundamental right of all human beings.
Article 2. Access to vaccines, medicine and treatment is guaranteed for all, regardless of financial means.
Article 3. Health systems must be resilient, coordinated and equitable in the face of pandemics.
Article 4. The intellectual property of vital medicines must not obstruct universal access.
Article 5. The institutions of wisdom are obliged to monitor and report on the indicators of worldwide health.
Protocol 9: Cybersecurity, Privacy and Data Sovereignty
Article 1. The privacy of individuals in the digital space is guaranteed.
Article 2. The collection, storage and use of personal data must be transparent, lawful and based on informed consent.
Article 3. Cyberattacks on critical infrastructure (hospitals, energy, water, elections) are regarded as international crimes.
Article 4. Data sovereignty must be defined on the basis of users' rights, the public commons and collective interests.
Article 5. The institutions of wisdom are obliged to monitor and report on the state of cybersecurity and privacy.
Protocol 10: Bioethics and Emerging Technologies
Article 1. Intervention in the human genome, cloning, artificial general intelligence and converging technologies (nano, bio, information, cognitive) require ethical frameworks and international oversight.
Article 2. Euthanasia, assisted suicide and end-of-life rights must be regulated within the framework of human dignity and freedom of choice.
Article 3. Abortion must be regulated on the basis of women's rights, public health and human dignity.
Article 4. Human experimentation without informed consent, human cloning and lethal autonomous weapons are prohibited.
Article 5. The institutions of wisdom are obliged to monitor and report on the challenges of bioethics.

Part VIII: The Addenda
Addenda A through J

Addendum A: Scope, Nature and Limits of Validity
This Pact is a normative and strategic document of worldwide scope, composed in order to set out wisdom-centered principles for individual, organizational, social and civilizational life.
This Charter is not, in and of itself, a legally binding document, unless it is voluntarily accepted, ratified or localized by states, institutions, organizations or human communities.
The validity of this Pact rests on informed acceptance, ethical commitment and collective reason, not on legal compulsion.
Addendum B: Position Within the Constellation of International Instruments
This Charter has been composed neither in conflict with existing worldwide documents, conventions and declarations nor in competition with them; rather, it regards itself as complementary to, and reinforcing of, humanity's shared efforts in the fields of human rights, sustainable development, the ethics of science and technology, and responsible governance.
This Charter seeks to add the layer of "wisdom" to this constellation, as the connector of ethics, knowledge, policymaking and foresight, and to provide a ground for deeper synergy among these fields.
Addendum C: The Operational Definition of Wisdom
In this Charter, wisdom is operationally defined as follows:
"The ability to recognize, evaluate and choose decisions and actions that take into account their ethical, systemic, long-term and intergenerational consequences, and that prefer sustainable collective good over short-term interests."
This definition is the basis for evaluating all the principles, policies and recommendations of this Pact.
Addendum D: The Levels of Realizing Wisdom
In this Charter, wisdom is understood and applied at four interconnected levels:
1.	Individual wisdom: self-awareness, responsibility, and ethics in personal decisions
2.	Institutional wisdom: responsible decision-making in organizations and institutions
3.	Social and civilizational wisdom: designing just, sustainable and humane structures
4.	Intergenerational wisdom: taking into account the rights of future generations and the habitability of the Earth
No level is complete without the others.
Addendum E: Monitoring, Evaluation and Review
In order to preserve the dynamism and effectiveness of this Pact:
1. The implementation of the Charter's principles may be examined through voluntary reports, wisdom-centered indicators and periodic evaluations.
1. The text of the Charter may be reviewed and updated every five years, or when worldwide necessity requires, by the consensus of collective reason.
1. This mechanism guarantees the Charter's vitality and its adaptation to future developments.

Addendum F: Institution-Building and Networking
This Pact may serve as the basis for, or inspiration of, the following:
1. Worldwide wisdom networks
1. Scientific-ethical consultative assemblies
1. Foundations, platforms or research centers
1. Educational and policymaking programs

The creation of such structures is voluntary, gradual and independent of legal obligations.
Addendum G: An Invitation to Worldwide Participation
This Charter invites universities, thinkers, policymakers, artists, social activists, civil society organizations and the citizens of the world to:
1. Take part in the worldwide dialogue on wisdom
1. Critique, complete and localize the principles of this Pact
1. Carry wisdom from the level of thought to the level of action

This Pact has no meaning without collective participation.
Addendum H: The Principle of Ideological Independence
This Charter is dependent on no particular religion, ideology, state, party or school, and defines itself as beyond the boundaries of belief and politics.
This Charter is a ground for shared human dialogue, not an instrument of intellectual or cultural supremacy.
Addendum I: The Principle of Evolution and Openness
This Charter is not a static text.
With the expansion of knowledge, the change of worldwide conditions and the deepening of human understanding of self and world, this Pact may evolve.
The spirit that governs it is continuous learning, epistemic humility and fidelity to collective reason.
Addendum J: The Digital Infrastructure and the Wisdom Ecosystem

1.	This platform is a ground for the worldwide dialogue on wisdom, the publication of documents, the monitoring of indicators, education, and the participation of citizens.

Part XI: Technical Appendix
Foundations of Reasoning, Limits and Horizons Ahead

1. Foundations of Logical and Scientific Reasoning
1. Systemic order: The Charter's claim about "world order" accords with complex systems theory and with the concept of "self-organization" in physics. The survival of civilization depends on harmony with the stable laws of the biosphere.
1. Sustainability on the basis of game theory: The commitment to peace and cooperation is not naive idealism but is designed on the basis of mathematical models of Nash equilibrium, in which, in long-term systems, cooperation is the only optimal strategy for the survival of all actors.
1. Ethics in technology: The necessity of joining knowledge and ethics is a technical response to the crisis of informational entropy and the existential risks of artificial intelligence (AI alignment).

2. Limitations and Uncertainties
We accept, with realism, that this Pact faces the following structural challenges:
1. Interpretative diversity: Concepts such as "wisdom" and "justice" bear different meanings in different cultural contexts. This Charter does not claim to offer a single, rigid version; it offers a ground for "continuous dialogue" toward shared understanding.
1. Systemic resistance: We are aware that many provisions of this Charter (especially in the fields of the environment and the distribution of power) conflict with current economic structures, which are built on short-term growth. The realization of this Charter requires a "paradigm shift" in worldwide governance.
1. Uncertainty in the digital transition: The speed of emerging technologies may transform some traditional definitions of the "human being" and of "free will." This document is therefore not a static text and requires periodic updates in line with contemporary knowledge.
1. Cognitive biases: The biological limits of the human brain in grasping vast and distant crises (such as climate change) are the greatest obstacle to implementing this Pact. We emphasize the role of "wisdom-centered education" in overcoming these biases.

3. Executive Conclusion
This Charter is an "open protocol." Our aim is to create a constellation in which "science" is the instrument, "reason" the commander, and "wisdom" the compass. We invite all thinkers, scientists and experts to take part in the evolutionary process of critiquing and completing this document.
"I know that the world is complex and I claim no miracle, but this is the most rational path that, with humanity's present knowledge, we can chart."
