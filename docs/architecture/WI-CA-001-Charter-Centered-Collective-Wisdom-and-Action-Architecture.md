# WI-CA-001 — Charter-Centered Collective Wisdom and Action Architecture

- **Status:** Proposed architectural baseline
- **Version:** 0.1.0
- **Date:** 2026-11-26
- **Normative parent:** Eternal Wisdom Charter / Wisdom Pact
- **Depends on:** WI-UX-003 (Wis / Dom / Land and WisdomLand gateway), WI-MA-001, WI-MR-001/002, Wisdom Tree, WQIE
- **Scope:** Conceptual, governance, product and operating architecture. This document does not activate financial, token, voting or production systems.

## 1. Purpose and architectural thesis

Wisdom Intercessor is to be developed as a Charter-centered, participatory environment in which people can understand, question, extend, test and apply the Eternal Wisdom Charter through individual and collective work.

The Charter is the normative and reflective foundation. It is not merely a static declaration, a membership form, a social-media policy or a product catalogue. Its principles are translated into rights, duties, participation rules, review procedures, institutional safeguards and measurable practices. Products and technical systems support those practices; they do not become sources of ultimate authority.

The operating cycle is:

**Understand → Question → Contribute → Deliberate → Decide → Collaborate → Apply → Evaluate → Learn → Revise.**

The platform must support both theoretical and practical wisdom: research, evidence, education and critique on one side; projects, services, experiments, stewardship and real-world outcomes on the other.

## 2. Constitutional and document hierarchy

The controlled hierarchy is:

1. **Eternal Wisdom Charter / منشور حکمت جاویدان** — foundational principles, human dignity, rights, responsibilities, ethical commitments and limits.
2. **Charter annexes and protocols** — operational interpretations, participation rules, identity and privacy rules, deliberation, voting, contribution recognition, reward principles, appeals and revision procedures.
3. **Master Whitepaper and Master Architecture** — system-wide structure, boundaries, trust model and interfaces.
4. **Master Registry** — authoritative inventory of concepts, products, systems, protocols, organizations and repositories, each with a lifecycle status and accountable owner.
5. **Domain and technical specifications** — detailed behavior, schemas, security, APIs, economics and implementation constraints.
6. **Operational policies and product implementations** — deployed workflows, service terms, moderation procedures and user interfaces.

A product, token, algorithm, administrator or majority vote cannot override Charter-level rights and safeguards. If a conflict is detected, the affected operation must be paused or constrained pending transparent review. A repository document or software release does not by itself amend the Charter or activate a real-world institution.

## 3. Three realms and the WisdomLand gateway

The experience remains **WisdomLand → Wis ↔ Dom ↔ Land**:

- **Wis / Inner Realm:** person, identity, self-knowledge, cognition, conscience, learning, intention, agency, rights and personal responsibility.
- **Dom / Connecting Realm:** relationships, communities, collaboration, knowledge exchange, institutions, governance, law, economy, culture, networks and digital infrastructure.
- **Land / Outer Realm:** Earth, ecosystems, living beings, geography, environment, resources, time, space and wider cosmos.

**WisdomLand** is the unified entry gateway, orientation and navigation layer, not a fourth realm. A participant may enter through any relevant activity, but the experience should make cross-realm effects visible. No realm is an isolated silo.

The Charter guides all three realms. WQIE may map cross-realm dependencies, impacts, conflicts, trade-offs and feedback, without asserting that social or ethical relationships are physically quantum.

## 4. The participant and WisdomID

**WisdomID** is the participant's persistent, privacy-respecting identity and account layer across the ecosystem. It should support:

- one participant identity across Wis, Dom and Land and across authorized products;
- profile, preferences, accessibility and consent settings;
- attribution of authorship, contribution, collaboration and provenance;
- portable participation history and earned non-financial recognition;
- role and permission management, including separate public, private and pseudonymous contexts;
- authentication, recovery, account security and abuse prevention;
- optional organization, project or community affiliations without conflating them with the person's identity.

WisdomID is not a public exposure mandate, a reputation score, a financial wallet, proof of legal identity by default, or a tool for tracking people across contexts without consent. Sensitive identity attributes must not be public by default. Identity assurance should be proportional to the risk of the action. Anonymous or pseudonymous participation should remain possible where accountability and law permit it.

A participant's identity, contribution record, voting eligibility, reputation, wallet address and legal identity are distinct data objects with explicit access rules. One must not silently imply another.

## 5. WisdomSpace and the participant journey

**WisdomSpace** is the participant's personal workspace and presence, not a fourth realm or a separate constitutional authority. It should bring together:

- personal dashboard and WisdomID controls;
- saved Charter principles, learning paths and research;
- authored content, drafts, publications and contribution history;
- collaborations, project roles, tasks and support commitments;
- deliberations, polls, consultation submissions and vote receipts where applicable;
- earned recognition, reward statements and transparent transaction records;
- privacy, consent, appeal, export and account controls.

A participant journey should be continuous: read a principle; ask a question; submit a source or critique; co-author a proposal; join a working group; participate in a consultation; support or undertake a project; report outcomes; receive an attributable record and, where eligible, recognition or reward.

## 6. Participation and contribution model

The platform must recognize diverse forms of contribution, not only publishing volume or financial activity. Contribution classes include:

1. **Knowledge creation:** articles, books, datasets, definitions, translations, educational material and creative works.
2. **Research and evidence:** hypotheses, methods, experiments, replication, field observations, source review and evidence synthesis.
3. **Critical contribution:** reasoned criticism, counterarguments, error reports, risk analysis, peer review and appeals.
4. **Deliberation and civic participation:** questions, proposals, consultation responses, public discussions, surveys and eligible referenda.
5. **Collaboration:** co-authorship, mentoring, teaching, moderation, facilitation, project coordination and review.
6. **Support:** financial or non-financial sponsorship, grants, equipment, time, expertise, infrastructure and in-kind assistance.
7. **Practical action:** implementation, community work, environmental stewardship, service delivery, prototypes and measurable field outcomes.
8. **Knowledge circulation:** sharing, translation, citation, lawful republication, distribution and accessibility improvements.

Every contribution should have an explicit type, author/attribution model, timestamp, version, provenance, licensing/permission state, review status and links to relevant Charter principles, domains and projects. Sharing alone must not be confused with original authorship; copied, automated or low-quality activity must not earn the same credit as verified original work.

## 7. Content, collaboration and publication lifecycle

The content lifecycle is:

**Draft → Submitted → Moderation / safety review → Peer or expert review (when required) → Accepted / disputed / needs revision → Published → Versioned → Corrected / superseded / archived.**

Not every contribution needs expert review. The review level should be proportionate to claims, risk and intended use. Scientific claims must distinguish evidence, interpretation, uncertainty and opinion. Legal, health, financial and safety-sensitive material requires appropriate scope labels and qualified review before being presented as authoritative guidance.

Collaboration must support transparent roles, contribution statements, licensing, attribution, conflict-of-interest declarations, project milestones, shared resources and dispute resolution. Contributors retain rights defined by the applicable license and law; platform participation must not silently transfer ownership.

## 8. Collective deliberation, polls and referenda

The system should distinguish:
- open discussion and informal polls;
- structured consultations and surveys;
- community decisions within a defined group or project;
- formal referenda under an explicitly approved governance procedure.

Every formal decision process must publish its question, scope, eligibility rules, electorate definition, quorum if any, method, duration, options, accessibility provisions, privacy model, audit method, conflict-of-interest rules, challenge/appeal route and legal status before opening.

A poll is not automatically binding. A platform vote is not a state election or a substitute for lawful public institutions. No vote may remove fundamental rights, silence minorities, retroactively change eligibility or bypass due process. Results must show participation counts and methodological limitations; avoid presenting simple majorities as universal wisdom.

## 9. Recognition, points and reward architecture

The platform may recognize eligible participation through distinct mechanisms. These must not be collapsed into a single score:

- **Contribution record:** an auditable description of what was done and its provenance.
- **Wisdom Points / participation points:** non-transferable recognition units for defined, eligible contributions; not money, not a claim on treasury assets and not automatically convertible.
- **Reputation signals:** contextual, explainable indicators based on review, reliability and contribution quality; never a universal human-worth score.
- **Grants and awards:** discretionary or rule-based support allocated through published criteria and independent review.
- **Revenue share or compensation:** contractual payment for eligible work, licensed content, services or project outcomes, subject to law, tax, consumer and labor requirements.
- **WeCoin:** a separately governed ecosystem economic instrument within WisdomChain, only if and when its legal, technical, economic, custody and risk controls are approved and implemented.

Rewardable activity may include original research, peer review, verified educational work, accepted improvements, meaningful criticism, translations, mentoring, project delivery, transparent support and participation in eligible deliberative processes. Merely clicking, mass posting, repetitive sharing, buying influence, coordinating fake accounts or voting many times must not create rewards.

Reward rules must be published before activity begins. Each reward event must record its rule version, eligible action, evidence, evaluator or automated decision path, calculation, funding source, appeal route and reversal/correction policy. Quality, integrity, usefulness, collaboration and real outcomes matter more than volume. Automated scoring must be explainable and contestable; human review must be available for consequential decisions.

No participant should be required to buy a token, connect a wallet or pay a fee to access basic Charter reading, learning, criticism or participation. Essential rights and civic voice cannot be pay-to-win. Financial rewards must never be used to manipulate votes or purchase agreement.

## 10. Product and system placement

Products are mapped to responsibilities, not promoted into constitutional authorities.

| Concept / product | Architectural responsibility | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **WisdomCore** | Cross-ecosystem core: shared principles, services, integration, policy enforcement interfaces and system coordination | Does not replace the Charter or participant agency |
| **WisdomID** | Identity, authentication, consent, attribution and portable participation record | Not a public dossier, reputation score or wallet by default |
| **WisdomSpace** | Personal workspace and participant interface | Not a fourth realm |
| **WisdomNetwork** | Communication, community, publishing, discovery and collaboration | Does not determine truth by popularity |
| **WisdomKnowledge** | Knowledge objects, research, education, evidence, review, citation and learning | Preserves provenance, disagreement and uncertainty |
| **Wisdom Governance & Justice** | Deliberation, proposals, rules, voting procedures, appeals and accountable decisions | No vote overrides fundamental rights or lawful process |
| **WisdomEconomy** | Economic coordination connecting the Wisdom Tree's roots, core and branches; registers economic rules, value flows, incentives, services and productive cooperation | Must not equate all value with token price or financial return |
| **WisdomChain** | Layer 2 product/subsystem for selected verifiable records, settlement or programmable coordination | Blockchain is optional infrastructure, not the source of legitimacy |
| **WeCoin** | Potential economic instrument / sap and fruit of the WisdomEconomy trunk, implemented within WisdomChain L2 | Separate governance, issuance, utility, risk, custody and legal review; not a universal reward for every action |
| **WisdomTreasury** | Accounting, treasury controls, budgeting, allocation, grants and financial reporting | Interacts with WeCoin through explicit contracts; does not own or govern WisdomChain |
| **WisdomGuard** | Security, privacy, integrity, abuse prevention, moderation support and resilience | Must provide due process, proportionality and appeal |
| **WQIE** | Cross-domain and cross-realm relationship analysis, evidence mapping, impact and trade-off analysis | Quantum-inspired modeling is not a claim of physical quantum behavior |

The Master Registry must classify each item as concept, product, subsystem, protocol, service, organization or repository candidate. A product name does not automatically require a separate repository.

## 11. WisdomEconomy and value flows

**WisdomEconomy is the trunk of the Wisdom Tree**: it connects foundations (roots), identity and principles (core), and productive domains (branches). It coordinates value creation, exchange, allocation and reinvestment across the ecosystem.

Its value model must recognize at least:
- knowledge and intellectual contribution;
- labor, expertise and services;
- collaboration and coordination;
- social and community benefit;
- environmental and stewardship outcomes;
- financial resources and productive assets;
- long-term capability, learning and resilience.

A value ledger should distinguish monetary value, non-monetary contribution, points, reputation and token balances. These are not interchangeable. No fixed conversion rate should be implied between them without an approved, legally reviewed policy and transparent accounting.

WisdomChain is a Layer 2 product/subsystem. WeCoin is associated with WisdomChain, not WisdomTreasury. WisdomTreasury may provide budgeting, accounting, grant allocation and reconciliation through explicit interfaces and contracts. No token issuance, exchange, custody, yield, investment promise or financial activation is authorized by this architecture document.

## 12. Governance, integrity and safeguards

Minimum safeguards:
- human dignity, equality, accessibility and non-discrimination;
- informed consent, data minimization, purpose limitation and privacy by default;
- transparent moderation, reasons, notice, appeal and independent review;
- separation of content moderation, reputation, reward decisions and voting administration;
- conflict-of-interest declarations and recusal;
- anti-sybil, anti-spam and anti-manipulation controls proportionate to risk;
- independent audits for consequential automated decisions;
- protection of minority views and reasoned dissent;
- transparent funding sources and reward budgets;
- no sale of votes, influence or privileged Charter interpretation;
- no coercive identity disclosure or compulsory wallet use;
- clear jurisdiction, age-appropriate access and safeguarding requirements;
- open revision process with version history and public rationale.

WisdomAI may assist with discovery, translation, synthesis, moderation triage and impact analysis, but it must disclose uncertainty, preserve source links and human review, and must not be the final authority on truth, rights, eligibility or consequential sanctions.

## 13. Measurement and evaluation

Use a balanced dashboard rather than one composite wisdom score. Possible dimensions:
- diversity and accessibility of participation;
- provenance and quality of evidence;
- review coverage and correction latency;
- collaboration and co-authorship;
- usefulness and adoption of knowledge;
- project completion and verified outcomes;
- fairness of reward allocation;
- appeal and reversal rates;
- privacy/security incidents;
- environmental and social effects;
- long-term learning and resilience.

Every metric requires a definition, data source, collection purpose, limitations, uncertainty and review schedule. Metrics must not become targets that encourage gaming or suppress dissent.

## 14. Implementation sequence

**Phase A — Normative alignment:** review Charter text and annexes; map each principle to rights, duties, procedures, affected realms and review questions. Publish a traceability matrix.

**Phase B — Registry and product boundaries:** register WisdomID, WisdomSpace, WisdomEconomy, WisdomChain, WeCoin, WisdomTreasury, WisdomNetwork, WisdomKnowledge, Wisdom Governance & Justice, WisdomGuard and WQIE with lifecycle states, owners, dependencies and interfaces.

**Phase C — Participation foundation:** implement account/consent, contribution types, provenance, content lifecycle, collaboration, reporting, moderation, appeal and export before introducing financial incentives.

**Phase D — Deliberation:** introduce consultations and non-binding polls first; formal referenda only after published eligibility, privacy, audit and due-process specifications.

**Phase E — Recognition and economy:** begin with transparent non-transferable recognition and grants; add compensation and any token functionality only after separate legal, economic, security and operational approval.

**Phase F — Evaluation and revision:** publish impact reports, independent reviews, dissent records and Charter amendment proposals.

## 15. Acceptance criteria

The architecture is ready for detailed specification when:
1. every product has a defined role and boundary;
2. every participation type has attribution, evidence and review rules;
3. rewards are separated from reputation, voting power and financial balances;
4. the Charter's rights and safeguards constrain every workflow;
5. Wis/Dom/Land mapping is explicit without forcing one-to-one mapping of products to realms;
6. WisdomID and WisdomSpace preserve privacy and participant control;
7. WisdomEconomy, WisdomChain, WeCoin and WisdomTreasury have distinct responsibilities and interfaces;
8. formal polls and referenda have transparent, auditable procedures;
9. implementation status is clearly separated from proposals;
10. all changes remain subject to documented review and approval.

## 16. Change control

This is a proposed architecture document. It does not amend the Charter, create legal rights by itself, activate a token, authorize financial activity, establish a binding electorate, publish WordPress pages or deploy production systems. Changes require versioned review, recorded rationale, impact analysis and approval under the repository's change-control process.
