The two Markdown editions show strong structural correspondence across the inspected material. Parts I–V appear in the same order; Part IV contains corresponding Principles 0–15 and nested sub-principles; Part V contains ten protocols with five articles each. This is a bounded semantic review, not a complete certification of equivalence. It does not establish that every sentence, table, footnote, or source-PDF element has been compared. No Charter text was changed.

## 4. Terminology and translation points requiring editorial review

These are review flags, not automatic corrections:

1. **Wisdom / خرد / عقل / حکمت.** The Persian edition uses conceptually related terms whose distinctions matter to the Charter's philosophical framework. The English edition often uses “wisdom” and sometimes “reason.” A controlled bilingual glossary should define each source term and its approved English rendering, then be applied consistently.
2. **“پیوند تاریخ، حال و آینده” / “The Bond of History, Present and Future.”** The English title communicates connection. Earlier wording such as “Unite the rift between past and future” would introduce a different metaphor. Use the actual current heading as the comparison baseline and keep alternate wording in the editorial issue log.
3. **“آب اطهر” / “The Most Pure Water”; “خاک احسان” / “The Soil of Beneficence”; “آتش دانش” / “The Fire of Knowledge”; “هوا – حکمت” / “Air – Wisdom.”** These are figurative and philosophically loaded labels. Their English renderings are broadly corresponding, but should be treated as controlled terms rather than casually paraphrased.
4. **Protocol 8 title:** Persian “سلامت جهانی و پاندمیها” is rendered “Worldwide Health and Pandemics.” The meaning is close; “Global Health and Pandemics” may be a more conventional editorial option, but any change requires owner approval.
5. **“معلولان” / “persons with disabilities.”** The English version uses person-first phrasing. This is a defensible translation choice; the bilingual glossary should record it rather than force literal word matching.

## 6. Document-control and extraction artifacts

The Persian Markdown contains repeated PDF page markers (for example, `<PARSED TEXT OF PAGE: ...>`) and page furniture. The English Markdown contains running headers, blank-line blocks, and repeated navigation glyphs. These are presentation/extraction artifacts rather than necessarily substantive differences, but they make automated comparison unreliable.

Contents-page numbers differ between editions. Page-number differences alone are not evidence of substantive divergence.

## 7. Part XIII standing statement

The inspected Persian and English closing statements both identify Hekmat Shafiei as the presenter/compiler/proposer and explicitly state that this standing does not imply representation, leadership, or political/legal authority. The English version's “compiler and proposer of the text” corresponds closely to the Persian formulation.

The digital infrastructure domain is named in both editions as wisdomintercessor.com.
