# WI-GOV-CHARTER-BILINGUAL-SEMANTIC-AUDIT-003

**Status:** Review evidence only; not an approval, ratification, legal opinion, or amendment  
**Edition:** Operational Edition v1.0 (2026), Persian and English Markdown renderings  
**Scope:** Parts VI–VIII (institutions, monitoring/accountability, Addenda A–J)  
**Sources:** `charter/CHARTER.md` and `charter/CHARTER-EN.md`, retrieved from the WisdomPact main branch for this review.

## 1. Executive finding

The inspected Persian and English passages show close structural and substantive correspondence across Parts VI–VIII. Five institutions appear in the same order with matching compositions and functions; Part VII contains the same eight indicator domains and the same reporting, accountability, and dispute-resolution mechanisms; Addenda A–J follow the same subject sequence and preserve the same central propositions in the passages reviewed.

No substantive omission or reordered institution, indicator domain, or addendum was identified in this bounded comparison. This is not a full certification of every line, table layout, or source-PDF element.

## 2. Part VI — Institutions of Wisdom

The five institutions align in order and general mandate:

1. World Wisdom Assembly — representative composition; adopts/revises Charter principles, oversees protocols, hears complaints, and issues annual reports.
2. Wisdom Council — sages and experts; interprets principles, advises the Assembly, monitors technologies and policies ethically, and promotes wisdom education.
3. Wisdom Court — independent judges, international jurists, and ethics experts; hears alleged violations, issues judgments described as non-binding but ethically binding, recommends redress, and develops wisdom-centered jurisprudence.
4. World Wisdom Network — universities, research centers, civil society organizations, and foundations; knowledge production, education/capacity-building, indicator monitoring, and international cooperation.
5. World Wisdom Fund — voluntary contributions; financing projects, supporting disadvantaged communities, investment in education/health/environment, and financial transparency.

### Review flags

- **“Non-binding but ethically binding”** is present in both languages. It is a deliberate normative formulation but can be misunderstood as a claim of enforceable legal authority. Preserve it as source language for now; any clarification should be handled as an approved Charter amendment, not silently introduced in translation.
- **Decision-making rule:** the Assembly uses “consensus of collective reason” with voting where necessary in both editions. Neither inspected text defines quorum, voting thresholds, representation rules, conflicts of interest, or appeal routes. These are governance-design gaps in the source, not translation discrepancies.
- **Institutional powers:** the text assigns complaint-handling and review functions but does not, in these passages, define jurisdictional limits, consent/acceptance conditions, enforcement mechanisms, or relationship to existing national courts and institutions. These require a separate implementation/legal-design review; they should not be inferred from the bilingual match.
- **Formatting:** Persian uses bullet lists; English extraction renders several lists as repeated “1.” markers. This appears to be a Markdown/PDF extraction or list-numbering artifact, not a substantive difference.

## 3. Part VII — Monitoring and Accountability

The indicator table aligns across eight domains:

| Domain | Shared indicator focus | Shared data-source description |
|---|---|---|
| Individual wisdom | Wisdom literacy, self-awareness, responsibility | National surveys |
| Institutional wisdom | Transparency, accountability, participation | Independent evaluations |
| Social wisdom | Justice, trust, solidarity | Development indicators |
| Intergenerational wisdom | Sustainability, climate justice, environmental debt | Scientific data |
| Environment | Air, water, soil quality; biodiversity | Satellite monitoring |
| Human rights | Freedom, equality, dignity | Reports of independent bodies |
| Economy | Inequality, poverty, employment | Statistical data |
| Technology | Access, ethics, innovation | Technical evaluations |

Reporting intervals match: annual global state-of-wisdom report; biennial protocol-progress report; comprehensive Charter review every five years.

The accountability categories match: public accessibility/transparency of reports, independent audit, individual and collective complaint mechanisms, and redress through restoration, apology, and compensation.

Dispute-resolution routes also match: mediation by the Wisdom Council, arbitration by the Wisdom Court, and referral to existing international bodies.

### Review flags

- The Persian source uses “دوساالنه” and the English source “Biennially”; these are equivalent.
- “Satellite monitoring” appears as the data source for environmental indicators in both editions. This is a source proposition, not evidence that a validated satellite-data methodology, access arrangement, or verification protocol currently exists.
- Neither edition, in the inspected text, defines indicator formulas, baselines, data governance, sampling, audit standards, uncertainty treatment, privacy safeguards, or independent verification procedures. These are operational specification gaps shared by both editions.
- “Arbitration” is the English rendering of “داوری”; the wording is aligned. The text does not define whether such arbitration is voluntary, binding, or subject to an existing legal framework.

## 4. Part VIII — Addenda A–J

The addenda sequence and principal content align:

| Addendum | Shared subject | Alignment finding |
|---|---|---|
| A | Scope, nature and limits of validity | Both state the Charter/Pact is not inherently legally binding absent voluntary acceptance, ratification, or localization |
| B | Position among international instruments | Both describe complementarity, not competition or conflict, and add wisdom as a connector among ethics, knowledge, policymaking, and foresight |
| C | Operational definition of wisdom | Both define recognition, evaluation, and choice with ethical, systemic, long-term, and intergenerational consequences, prioritizing sustainable collective good over short-term interests |
| D | Levels of realizing wisdom | Same four levels: individual, institutional, social/civilizational, intergenerational |
| E | Monitoring, evaluation, review | Voluntary reports/indicators/periodic evaluations; five-year or necessity-based review by consensus |
| F | Institution-building and networking | Same voluntary institutional/network/educational possibilities |
| G | Worldwide participation | Same invitation to universities, thinkers, policymakers, artists, civil society, and citizens; critique, complete, localize, and translate wisdom into action |
| H | Ideological independence | Independence from any particular religion, ideology, state, party, or school; shared dialogue rather than supremacy |
| I | Evolution and openness | Non-static text, evolving with knowledge and conditions; continuous learning, epistemic humility, collective reason |
| J | Digital infrastructure and Wisdom Ecosystem | Same domain and platform functions/principles, including transparency, openness, security, multilingual accessibility, and auditable public documents/reports/indicators |

### Review flags

1. **Addendum A terminology:** both editions use “Pact” and “Charter” within the same addendum. The alternation is present in both languages and appears intentional or inherited from the document's dual title; define the relationship in the glossary/document-control policy rather than normalize it silently.
2. **Addendum B scope:** “worldwide documents/instruments” is translated consistently. Neither language specifies which instruments are included; this is a scope-definition issue, not a translation mismatch.
3. **Addendum E review mechanism:** the five-year interval and “worldwide necessity” trigger align. Neither version defines who determines necessity, how a review is initiated, or how consensus is measured.
4. **Addendum J domain rendering:** the Persian Markdown extraction displays the domain in reversed order as `com.wisdomintercessor`, likely due to right-to-left bidirectional rendering. The English version displays `wisdomintercessor.com`. This should be treated as a formatting/rendering defect and verified in the source DOCX/PDF before any content correction.
5. **Addendum J public disclosure:** both versions state that all documents, reports, and wisdom indicators must be published publicly and audibly/auditably. The wording is aligned, but implementation must reconcile this broad publication commitment with privacy, consent, security, confidentiality, and applicable law. The current text does not spell out those exceptions or safeguards.

## 5. Cross-cutting findings

- No material language divergence was identified in the inspected Parts VI–VIII.

- English list numbering and Persian right-to-left domain rendering are formatting concerns.
- Institutional jurisdiction, indicator methodology, arbitration status, review initiation, and public-data safeguards remain undefined in the inspected source text.
