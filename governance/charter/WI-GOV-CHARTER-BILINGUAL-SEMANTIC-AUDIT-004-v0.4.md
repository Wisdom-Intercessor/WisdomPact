# WI-GOV-CHARTER-BILINGUAL-SEMANTIC-AUDIT-004
## Parts IX–XIII — Persian–English semantic and structural review

**Status:** Review evidence only; not a legal opinion, translation certification, or approval of the Charter.  
**Sources inspected:** `charter/CHARTER.md` and `charter/CHARTER-EN.md`, current `main` after PR #34.  
**Scope:** Parts IX–XIII as represented in the two Markdown editions. Original PDF/DOCX source comparison and rendered-page QA are not included in this audit.

## 1. Executive result

The inspected Persian and English texts are substantially aligned in the sequence and principal content of Parts IX–XIII. The Covenant has seven corresponding commitments; the Technical Appendix has three corresponding sections; the Ten-Year Roadmap has ten rows; and the Presenter’s Standing contains the same express disclaimer against claims of representation, leadership, or political/legal authority.

No substantive omission or reordered section was identified in the inspected Markdown text. The following matters require editorial or governance attention before treating the editions as publication-final.

## 2. Part-by-part findings

### Part IX — The Hymn of Praise

- Both editions follow the same thematic progression: the Creator; Earth; humanity; Water; Soil; Fire; Air/Wind; spacetime; reason; justice; freedom and dignity; truth; stewardship of the Charter; and future generations.
- The English title uses “The Hymn of Praise” and “The Hymn of the Eternal Wisdom Charter”; the Persian title is «ستایشنامه» and «ستایش منشور حکمت جاویدان». This is a reasonable title correspondence, though not a word-for-word rendering.
- The closing invocation and the aspiration for a world of justice, reason/wisdom, peace, freedom, kindness and truth are present in both.
- Editorial note: retain a controlled glossary for «خرد»، «عقل» and «حکمت»; English renderings vary by context (reason, wisdom). This is a cross-document terminology issue, not an omission in Part IX.

### Part X — The Covenant

- The opening declaration and all seven covenant headings correspond in sequence: Lord of Wisdom; Earth/Nature/Roots of Life; Human Being/Individual/Society; Justice/Truth; Peace/Coexistence/Universal Humanity; Knowledge/Reason/Eternal Wisdom; Future Generations.
- The principal pledges under each heading correspond in substance, including the Earth and nature commitments, dignity and rights, justice and law, peace and diversity, ethical science/technology, and intergenerational responsibility.
- The closing formula and prayer are present in both editions.
- Editorial note: the Persian text uses «سوگند یاد می‌کنیم»، «متعهد می‌شویم» and «پیمان می‌بندیم» in different subsections; the English similarly alternates “swear”, “pledge” and “make this compact”. This is not a material mismatch, but a translation style sheet could standardize the intended distinction between oath, pledge and covenant.

### Part XI — Technical Appendix

- The three-part structure aligns: foundations of logical/scientific reasoning; limitations and uncertainties; executive conclusion.
- The same three foundations appear in both editions: systemic order/self-organization; cooperation and Nash equilibrium/game theory; and ethics in technology/AI alignment.
- The same four limitations appear: interpretative diversity; systemic resistance; uncertainty in digital transition; and cognitive biases.
- Both editions conclude with the “open protocol” framing and the science/reason/wisdom compass metaphor.
- **Substantive verification flag:** the statement that cooperation is “the only optimal strategy” in long-term systems is a broad theoretical assertion. Its validity depends on model assumptions and conditions; neither language edition supplies citations, a model specification, or boundary conditions. This is a source-level evidentiary issue, not a translation discrepancy.
- **Technical verification flag:** the references to informational entropy and AI alignment are retained in both editions but are not defined or sourced in the appendix. Expert review and citations are needed before presenting these as established technical conclusions.
- Translation note: Persian parenthetical English technical terms are sometimes displayed in reversed order by RTL extraction (for example, “Self-organization” and “Nash equilibrium”). Verify the actual rendered PDF/DOCX before editing source wording.

### Part XII — The Ten-Year Roadmap

- Both editions contain ten annual milestones and the same responsible-party assignments.
- The milestones correspond in sequence: foundational declaration; Wisdom Council and Wisdom Court; indicators; Wisdom Network; Wisdom Fund; protocol implementation in 50 countries; first global wisdom report; five-year review; expansion to 100 countries; second report and comprehensive review.
- **Planning coherence flag:** the roadmap schedules the first five-year review in Year 8, although Addendum E describes review every five years. The two language editions share this timing, so it is not a translation error. The schedule needs an explicit rationale or correction through the Charter’s approved amendment process.
- **Authority and feasibility flag:** several milestones assign action to bodies that are proposed within the Charter itself (for example, the World Wisdom Assembly). The roadmap does not state the prerequisite process for constituting those bodies, the legal/voluntary basis for country participation, or the conditions for claiming implementation in 50/100 countries. These are implementation design gaps, not evidence that the institutions already exist.

### Part XIII — The Presenter’s Standing and the Digital Infrastructure

- Both editions identify the presenter/compiler/proposer and name wisdomintercessor.com as the digital infrastructure.
- Both explicitly state that this standing does not imply representation, leadership, or political/legal authority. The disclaimer is substantively aligned.
- **RTL/rendering issue:** the Persian Markdown extraction displays the domain as `com.wisdomintercessor`, while the English edition displays `wisdomintercessor.com`. This is consistent with bidirectional-text rendering behavior, but the actual Persian PDF/DOCX must be visually checked to ensure the URL is displayed and copied correctly. Do not reverse the stored canonical URL merely to compensate for a text-extraction artifact.
- The Part XIII label differs slightly: Persian «جایگاه ارائه‌دهنده و زیرساخت دیجیتال» and English “The Presenter's Standing and the Digital Infrastructure”. Meaning is aligned.

## 3. Cross-cutting editorial and governance actions

1. Establish a bilingual terminology register for خرد / عقل / حکمت and recurring technical/legal terms.
2. Resolve the Year 8 versus five-year review schedule through a documented editorial decision; do not silently alter either edition.
3. Add citations and scope conditions for the technical claims in Part XI, especially the game-theory/Nash equilibrium assertion and AI-alignment discussion.
4. Define the prerequisites and evidence standard for roadmap milestones involving the proposed institutions and implementation in countries.
5. Perform visual QA on Persian RTL output, especially URLs, numbered lists, table columns, punctuation and mixed Persian/Latin technical expressions.
6. Compare the Markdown editions against the source PDF and both DOCX/PDF release files in a separate artifact-level audit. This report does not certify those files.

## 4. Boundaries

- No Charter wording was changed by this audit.
- This review does not certify legal enforceability, scientific validity, institutional existence, country endorsement, or implementation readiness.
- The findings are limited to the inspected Markdown editions. PDF/DOCX rendering and source-file fidelity remain pending.
